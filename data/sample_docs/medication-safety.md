# Reading medicine labels and asking safer questions

This plain-language guide summarizes general information from the U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA). It is not a review of any one person's medicines.

## What a medicine label can tell you

A nonprescription medicine label includes active ingredients, uses, warnings, directions, and other information. Read the label each time you use a medicine because warnings or directions may change. The directions and warnings are specific to that product.

## Why a complete medicine list matters

Tell your doctor and pharmacist about all prescription medicines, over-the-counter medicines, vitamins, supplements, and herbal products you use. Medicines can interact with other medicines, foods, drinks, supplements, or health conditions. Some different products can also contain the same active ingredient.

## Where to take a personal question

Ask a pharmacist or the clinician who prescribed the medicine about your own medicines, possible interactions, side effects, and what to do if you miss a dose. Do not start, stop, skip, combine, or change a medicine dose based on this AI demo. Follow your prescription and the current product label, and confirm questions with a qualified health professional.
