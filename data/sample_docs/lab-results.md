# Understanding a lab-result flag

This plain-language guide summarizes information from MedlinePlus, a service of the U.S. National Library of Medicine.

## A lab result is one piece of the picture

Lab tests can provide useful information, but they do not give a complete picture of a person's health by themselves. A health care provider considers results together with symptoms, medical history, an examination, family history, and sometimes other tests.

## What a reference range means

A reference range is the range shown by a laboratory for a particular test. Ranges can differ between laboratories, test methods, units, and groups of people. Use the range printed on the report rather than comparing a number with a range found elsewhere.

A result outside the range may or may not mean there is a health problem. Healthy people sometimes have results outside a reference range. A result inside the range does not always rule out a health problem. A qualified health care provider can explain what a result means for an individual.

## Helpful next step

For a personal result, contact the clinician who ordered the test and ask what the result means in the context of your health and whether follow-up is needed. This demo cannot diagnose or interpret a person's lab result.
